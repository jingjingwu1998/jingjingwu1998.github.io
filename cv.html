Research Profile

I am a Ph.D. student conducting cardiovascular research in Dr. Da-Zhi Wang's laboratory at the University of South Florida.

My research focuses on understanding the mitochondrial mechanisms underlying cardiovascular disease, particularly how obesity contributes to heart failure. I integrate preclinical disease models, cardiac physiological assessments, molecular biology, and transcriptomic analyses to investigate disease mechanisms and identify potential therapeutic targets.

Beyond academic research, I have experience in pharmaceutical business development, commercial strategy, and biotechnology investment. I am also passionate about mentoring junior researchers and fostering interdisciplinary scientific collaboration.

Research Focus

  • Mitochondrial Biology and Cardiac Metabolism: Investigating mitochondrial protein translation, respiratory function, and metabolic adaptation in cardiovascular disease.
  • Cardiovascular Disease and Heart Failure: Studying HFpEF, HFrEF, and cardiac remodeling through preclinical disease models and integrative physiological phenotyping.
  • Computational Genomics and Translational Research: Integrating RNA-seq and Hi-C analyses to characterize molecular responses and investigate candidate therapeutic pathways.

Research experience

University of South Florida

Graduate Researcher | Cardiovascular and Molecular Medicine

January 2024 – Present · Tampa, Florida, USA

Investigate the molecular and mitochondrial mechanisms underlying cardiovascular disease by integrating genetically engineered mouse models, preclinical disease modeling, physiological phenotyping, molecular biology, and bioinformatics.

  • Genetically Engineered Mouse Models: Utilize global and cardiomyocyte-specific knockout (KO) and knockdown (KD) mouse models, including tamoxifen-inducible Myh6-MerCreMer (MCM) systems, to investigate gene function and disease mechanisms in the heart.
  • Cardiovascular Disease Modeling: Design and execute preclinical studies of heart failure with preserved and reduced ejection fraction (HFpEF and HFrEF) using obesogenic diets, myocardial infarction (MI), transverse aortic constriction (TAC), and isoproterenol administration via mini-osmotic pumps.
  • In Vivo miRNA Modulation and Therapeutic Intervention: Prepare miRNA inhibitor–Lipofectamine complexes for intracardiac administration in mouse models to investigate miRNA-mediated regulation of cardiac function and disease-associated molecular pathways.
  • Cardiac and Metabolic Phenotyping: Evaluate cardiac structure, function, and exercise capacity using echocardiography, cardiac MRI, and treadmill exercise testing. Assess systemic metabolic phenotypes through DEXA, glucose tolerance testing, and lipid profiling.
  • Histology and Tissue Imaging: Design histological and immunostaining studies and analyze imaging data from cardiac and metabolic tissues, including white adipose tissue (WAT), brown adipose tissue (BAT), and liver. Interpret histological (H&E and PSFG) and immunostaining (IHC and IF) results to assess tissue morphology, fibrosis, protein expression, and pathological remodeling.
  • Mitochondrial Structure and Function: Characterize cardiac mitochondrial abundance and ultrastructure using transmission electron microscopy (TEM), assess mitochondrial respiratory function through Seahorse extracellular flux analysis of heart-derived preparations, and evaluate citrate synthase and mitochondrial Complex I enzymatic activities using commercial assay kits.
  • Molecular and Bioinformatic Analyses: Perform RNA and protein analyses, including RT-qPCR for miRNAs and mRNAs and Western blotting, to investigate molecular pathways associated with cardiac dysfunction. Conduct RNA-seq and Hi-C bioinformatics analyses under the supervision of Dr. Mingxiang Teng at Moffitt Cancer Center.
